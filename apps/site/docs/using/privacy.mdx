---
title: "What your organization can see"
description: "The honest list — what reaches the organization, who can read it, and what never leaves your machine"
---

The agent runs on your machine, but the **record** belongs to your organization. That is the whole
design, and it cuts both ways: your work survives a lost laptop, and your work is your employer's.

This page is the specific version of that sentence.

## What reaches the organization

| | Reaches the organization | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Your conversations** | Yes — in full | Every message, both sides, plus attachments and generated files |
| **Files in your agent workspace** | Yes | Everything under `~/.wfc/workspace` that syncs — see below |
| **Files elsewhere on your computer** | **No** | The agent reads and edits them in place; nothing outside the workspace is uploaded |
| **Your settings** | Yes | One row, sealed at rest, including your saved variables |
| **What you spent** | Yes | Who, when, which model, how many tokens, how long, what it cost, what was decided |
| **That you searched the web** | Yes | **Never the query text** |
| **Your device PIN** | **No** | It never leaves the machine and cannot be read by anyone |
| **What the browser extension does** | **No** | It runs over `localhost` and has no cloud endpoint |
| **Your voice audio** | **No** | Speech-to-text and text-to-speech run on your machine |

The workspace folders that sync are the agent's own: `files`, `uploads`, `brain` (its memory and
your standing instructions), `voice`, `speech`, `screenshots`, `downloads`, `usage` and the
extension's per-conversation browser trail. Your undo snapshots, the rotating app logs and the
search index stay on the device.

## Who can read it

Roles are a fixed hierarchy — `owner`, `admin`, `support`, `employee` — and the rules are one table
the server checks on every request, not a matter of which buttons a client draws.

| | An admin or owner | Support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your name, role, team, devices | Yes | Yes |
| Your usage totals and the leaderboard | Yes | Yes |
| **Your conversation transcripts** | **Yes — and the read is audited** | **No** |
| **Your settings row (with your variables)** | **Yes — and the read is audited** | **No** |
| Change your role, caps or capabilities | Yes | No |

Support is the view-only tier for **operations**, not a licence to read your words. The two reads
that expose your actual work — your transcripts and your settings — are admin-tier, blocked for
support, blocked against an owner unless the reader is an owner too, and each one writes an audit
row naming who read what and when.

<Note>
`config.view` and `conversation.view` are the only audited **reads** in the whole platform. Every
other audit row is a change. That asymmetry is deliberate: reading your work is the thing worth a
permanent record.
</Note>

## What is never recorded anywhere

- **The router never logs a prompt or a reply.** It reads the stream only far enough to find the
  token counts. Your transcripts are at the organization because your desktop *synced the
  conversation* — a different, visible act — not because the model lane kept a copy.
- **No search query text.** The search row records that a search happened, how long it took and what
  it cost. What you asked is not stored.
- **Nothing identifying goes upstream.** The model host receives your request body, the model id and
  the organization's key. Not your name, not your email, not your user id.
- **No provider key is ever on your device**, so nothing you run can spend outside the lane.

## When the agent can see your screen

Computer use is the one capability that watches you rather than your files. While it is able to
capture the screen, your display carries a **blue edge glow and a centred pill** saying so. The
indicator is raised before the first capture and cannot survive the end of a turn — it is enforced
three ways, including a failsafe that clears it if the model fails to.

If the glow is not there, nothing is being captured. Screenshots the agent does take land in your
workspace, which means they sync like any other file.

## What everyone in the organization can see about you

The [leaderboard](/using/surfaces#the-leaderboard) is open to every signed-in person: your name,
tokens spent, conversations held and agentic tasks. It carries **no cost figure** — money stays in
the admin layer.

## Leaving, and being removed

- **Signing out** drains anything unsent, revokes the session, then wipes the local cache. Nothing
  of yours is left on a machine you are leaving.
- **Being removed** is two separate acts your admin performs: suspending the account stops future
  sign-ins, revoking sessions kills the ones that already exist — closing your phone's link and
  dropping its push registration immediately. The record stays with the organization.
- **Deleting a conversation** tombstones it immediately; its records and its archived copy are
  purged 30 days later.

## The short version

Treat the agent the way you would treat your work email: it is a company system, the company can
read it, and the reads are logged. What it adds over email is that it runs as **you**, on your
machine, with your permissions — so the files it touches outside the workspace are yours alone, and
the only things that travel are the ones this page names.
