The short answer: free agentic search is enough for *staying informed*, but not for *getting things done*. If you want to be told things, Google's agents in Search now handle it. If you want an agent to act on your accounts — sort your email, book, draft replies, run a task — you need a dedicated agent. Here's how to tell which side you're on.

## What "agentic search" actually is

Google Search has been quietly becoming agentic. At I/O 2026, Google announced that AI Mode now lets you create and manage multiple agents that monitor topics for you — like one that keeps you "updated when any of my favorite athletes announce sneaker collabs." [WIRED's framing](https://www.wired.com/story/google-search-goes-agentic-and-doesnt-need-you-anymore/) was blunt: Search went agentic and "doesn't need you anymore." You set a watchlist, and it keeps watch.

The key detail: these are *monitoring and answering* agents. They search, summarize, and notify — in the background, for free, powered by the new Gemini 3.5 Flash default in AI Mode. That's genuinely useful, and it's now the default experience for most people.

## What a dedicated agent does differently

A dedicated agent — the kind you install or subscribe to — doesn't just watch. It *acts*. It connects to your inbox, calendar, and files, and completes multi-step tasks on your behalf with your permission: triage your email, prep for a meeting, book the reservation, fill the spreadsheet. That's a different category from a search agent, and the free search version isn't a substitute for it.

| | Agentic Search (AI Mode) | Dedicated AI Agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it does | Watches topics, summarizes, answers | Completes multi-step tasks, uses tools |
| Access to your data | None beyond your search history | Email, calendar, files, accounts |
| Actions | No — it informs | Yes — it does, with your approval |
| Where it runs | Google's servers | Your choice — cloud or local |
| Cost | Free | Free to paid |
| Best for | "Keep me updated," research, quick answers | "Do this for me," recurring tasks |

## The honest test

Ask one question: **am I delegating a task, or subscribing to a feed?**

- If your need is "tell me when X changes," "summarize this," or "find the latest on Y" — agentic search is enough. Don't pay for an agent to do that.
- If your need is "sort my inbox and draft replies," "book the table for Thursday," "track my expenses," or "do this every morning without me asking" — that's a real agent's job, because it needs your accounts and the ability to act.

There's a middle zone worth naming. An [agent that runs while you sleep](https://wolffi.sh/blog/schedule-ai-agent-automations) or [automates a recurring workflow](https://wolffi.sh/start#morning-briefing) is exactly where search agents fall short — they can't do the work, only report on it.

## The takeaway

Don't buy a dedicated agent to replace free search — that's a waste. Do use one when the job stops being "tell me" and becomes "do it." The line is delegation, not intelligence. Agentic search informs; a real agent acts. Pick based on which of those you actually want from your mornings.

One more thing worth knowing: the free search agents keep your data on Google's servers, while a [dedicated agent can live on your own machine](https://wolffi.sh/blog/local-first-vs-cloud-ai-assistant) if that matters to you. That's the other thing free isn't always.
