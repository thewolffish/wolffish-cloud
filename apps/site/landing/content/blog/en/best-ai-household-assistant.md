The best AI household assistant in 2026 is the one that treats your home as a shared system rather than a personal to-do list. Ohai is the smarter pick if your household already lives in shared calendars and forwarded emails; Ollie wins if you want strict per-person task assignment. Both are built for the same job — keeping a family's schedules, meals, and errands from falling through the cracks — but they approach it differently.

## What a household agent is not

First, the boundary. A household agent is not just a smarter calendar. Calendar agents and travel agents and meeting agents all optimize a single person's time. A household agent has a different unit of work: the whole family. It tracks appointments across multiple people, plans meals, assigns chores, and keeps the shared list honest.

That's the real product. The value isn't a better schedule; it's not having to be the person who remembers everything for everyone.

## The two leaders, compared

Two names keep coming up for households, and they're worth putting side by side.

| Tool | How it works | Best for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ohai** | App-plus-SMS household manager; you forward emails and upload photos/PDFs | Parents juggling school schedules, meals, and errands | Relies on you feeding it context; shared workspace needs buy-in |
| **Ollie** | Assistant for shared tasks, calendars, and meals across the family | Homes that want per-person task assignment | Smaller ecosystem; fewer integrations so far |

The difference that decides it is where the information comes from. Ohai is designed around your input — you forward the school email, snap the permission slip, and it folds it into the household list. That makes it a strong fit for a home where the work already flows through email and photos. Ollie leans more on shared task and calendar structures, which works when you want clear ownership of responsibilities.

## What actually makes one work

No matter which you pick, a few things separate a household assistant you keep from one you delete. These are the criteria that matter, not the feature list:

- **Shared, not solo.** The whole point is that more than one person uses it. If it only works for your personal account, it's a calendar app with extra steps.
- **It takes input the way your family actually communicates.** If your family lives in forwarded emails and photos, pick a tool that ingests those. If it lives in a shared list, pick one that's list-first.
- **Per-person accountability.** Chores and tasks need an owner. The more clearly a tool can assign and track responsibility, the less you personally carry.
- **Cost per household, not per seat.** One good household assistant beats three subscriptions. A tool that also handles [your calendar](https://wolffi.sh/blog/best-ai-calendar-agent) and [family planning](https://wolffi.sh/blog/best-ai-travel-agent-2026) earns its price.

## The honest limit

Household agents are real, and they're useful, but they're not magic. They still rely heavily on the household feeding them accurate context — a family that doesn't forward the right emails or update the shared list gets a tool that's guessing. And because household logistics touches private details about your kids and your routines, [where the data lives](https://wolffi.sh/blog/ai-agent-liability) is a real consideration. A household agent that processes everything in the cloud is convenient; one that keeps sensitive family details closer to home is something to weigh if that matters to you.

The practical expectation to set: a household agent removes the *remembering*, not the *doing*. You still forward the email, still add the note, still confirm the appointment. What you stop doing is holding all of it in your head.

## The takeaway

If your household is drowning in shared logistics, an agent built for families is genuinely worth trying. Pick based on how your home already coordinates: Ohai for email-and-photo households, Ollie for list-first ones with clear task owners. Then give it a real two-week run with honest input, and judge it on whether the family stops relying on you to remember everything.

The best household assistant isn't the one with the longest feature list — it's the one your family will actually feed and trust. That's the only metric that ends up mattering.
