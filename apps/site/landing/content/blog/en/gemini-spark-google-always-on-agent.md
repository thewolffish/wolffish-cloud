Gemini Spark is Google's always-on personal AI agent — an assistant that lives in the cloud and keeps working through your email, calendar, and travel plans even when your phone is in your pocket. It debuted as a beta perk at Google I/O in May 2026, and is now rolling out to $20/month Google AI Pro subscribers in the US, with more countries and languages following.

## What Gemini Spark actually is

Most AI assistants only work when you open the app and ask a question. Spark is different: it's an *ambient* agent that runs continuously on Google's cloud, watching for work it can do and doing it before you think to ask. The goal is a helper that doesn't wait for a prompt — it notices your flight changed, flags the important email, and drafts the reply while you're doing something else.

Under the hood, Spark runs on [Gemini 3.5](https://gemini.google/overview/agent/spark/) and plugs directly into Google's own apps rather than reaching them through third-party connectors. That native access is the whole reason it can be more capable than a bolt-on assistant: it reads and writes the same Gmail, Calendar, Docs, and Drive you already use.

## What it can do

Google has positioned Spark around the tasks that eat real time in a normal week:

- **Email** — sort, summarize, and draft replies before you open the app.
- **Calendar** — schedule, reschedule, and prep you for meetings.
- **Travel** — track flights and rebook when plans change.
- **Expenses** — gather receipts and organize them.
- **Documents** — edit shared Docs and Slides with you, or enhance files with Canvas.

The integration list tells the story of why it matters:

| App | What Spark does there |
| --- | --- |
| Gmail | Triage, summaries, draft replies |
| Calendar | Scheduling, prep, reminders |
| Docs / Sheets / Slides | Co-editing, drafting, summarizing |
| Drive | Find and organize files |
| Chrome | Act inside the browser on your behalf |
| Android | Replace Google Assistant for on-device tasks |

It also connects to a handful of MCP partners, which extends it beyond Google's walls — a signal of where the platform is heading.

## What it costs

Spark's rollout has been a price story as much as a product story:

| Tier | Monthly price | Spark access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google AI Free | $0 | No |
| Google AI Pro | $19.99 | Yes (US first, expanding) |
| Google AI Ultra | $100+ | Yes (from launch) |

The $100 Ultra barrier was the early criticism — a 24/7 agent is a luxury if it costs four figures a year. Moving Spark down to the $20 Pro tier ([first reported in July 2026](https://www.pcmag.com/news/googles-gemini-spark-agentic-ai-assistant-now-rolling-out-to-more-users)) is Google making the product real rather than a demo. Expect the pricing ladder to keep flattening as [every big lab races to put an agent in your day](https://wolffi.sh/blog/best-personal-ai-agent-2026).

## The always-on shift

Spark matters less for any single feature than for what it represents: the move from assistants that *answer* to agents that *run*. The [old Google Assistant is being replaced by Spark](https://www.lifehacker.com/tech/it-just-got-cheaper-to-try-googles-agentic-ai-gemini-spark) on Android, which means hundreds of millions of people are about to have a real agent by default, not as something they installed.

That's the quiet milestone. A few years ago you had to be a power user to run an agent; now one ships inside the default assistant on the world's most common phone OS.

## Control and privacy — the honest caveats

Two things keep Spark from being a black box:

1. **It asks before big actions.** Spark proposes; you approve consequential steps rather than it acting unilaterally.
2. **Your data stays in Google's cloud.** That's convenient, but it's the opposite of local-first. Everything Spark sees — your mail, your calendar, your documents — lives on Google's servers.

That second point is the real trade-off, and it's why a [local-first vs cloud choice](https://wolffi.sh/blog/local-first-vs-cloud-ai-assistant) isn't just a technical footnote. Spark is the strongest argument yet for the cloud approach: maximum capability, minimum setup. An agent like [Wolffish](https://wolffi.sh/start) takes the opposite bet — run on your machine, keep your memory yours, and call a cloud model only when you need the horsepower. Neither is wrong; they're different answers to the same question of where your life's data should live.

## Takeaway

Gemini Spark is the first always-on personal agent to reach a mainstream price point — and it's about to arrive on hundreds of millions of Android phones. If you live in Google's apps and don't mind your agent living in Google's cloud, it's worth trying at $20/month. If you'd rather your agent run where your data is, [start local](https://wolffi.sh/start#morning-briefing) and grow from there. Either way, the era of the agent you open is ending; the era of the agent that's already running has begun.
